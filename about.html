About

Hello, I'm Franklina.

I am a doctoral researcher in Applied Statistics at the University of Northern Colorado. My research interests sit at the intersection of statistical methodology and real-world questions, particularly in causal inference, machine learning, public health, education, and policy research.

I am especially interested in working with complex and large-scale data to understand patterns, evaluate interventions, and produce evidence that can contribute to meaningful decision-making. I enjoy the process of moving from a substantive research question to a thoughtful statistical analysis and then communicating the results in a way that is both rigorous and understandable.

My broader goal is to continue developing as an applied statistician while contributing to research that connects strong quantitative methods with practical impact.

Research Approach

I am drawn to research questions where statistical methods can help clarify complex real-world relationships. My work emphasizes careful research design, transparent analysis, reproducible workflows, and clear communication. I am particularly interested in methods that help researchers move beyond describing patterns in data toward understanding the effects of policies, programs, and interventions.
