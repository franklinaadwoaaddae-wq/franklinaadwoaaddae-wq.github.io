A little more about me

About

Applied statistics with a focus on meaningful, real-world questions.

I am a doctoral researcher in Applied Statistics at the University of Northern Colorado. My research interests include causal inference, machine learning, public health, education, and policy-focused research.

I am particularly interested in using statistical methods and large-scale data to investigate questions with practical implications. I enjoy working at the intersection of methodological rigor and applied research, where careful statistical analysis can help make complex information more useful and understandable.

I also enjoy learning new methods, exploring new ideas, and finding clear ways to communicate what complex data can tell us.

Research Approach

I value thoughtful research design, transparent analysis, reproducible workflows, and careful interpretation. My goal is to use strong statistical methods while keeping the substantive research question at the center of the analysis.

I am especially drawn to projects that combine methodological depth with practical impact and provide opportunities to learn, collaborate, and communicate statistical findings clearly.

Beyond Research

Outside of research, I enjoy staying active, exploring new places, spending time on creative projects, and experiencing life in Colorado. I value curiosity, balance, continuous learning, and opportunities to grow both professionally and personally.
