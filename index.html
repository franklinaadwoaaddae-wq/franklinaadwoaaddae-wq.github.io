I am a PhD student in Applied Statistics with research interests in causal inference, public health, machine learning, and policy-focused research. I enjoy turning complex datasets into meaningful statistical evidence that can inform real-world decisions.

My research interests center on the application of modern statistical methods to substantive problems, particularly questions involving health policy, preventive care, education, and large-scale observational data.

I am especially interested in causal inference, statistical computing, reproducible research, and the growing intersection between machine learning and applied statistical research.

Outside of research, I enjoy staying active, exploring new places, and spending time on creative projects. I value curiosity, continuous learning, and using quantitative methods to address questions with practical impact.

Master's Thesis

Did Medicaid Expansion Increase Preventive Care?

This project evaluates whether Medicaid expansion was associated with increased preventive care utilization using BRFSS data and modern causal inference methods.

  • Difference-in-Differences
  • Event Study Analysis
  • Double Machine Learning
  • Large-scale BRFSS data
  • Reproducible statistical analysis

The research focuses on policy-relevant evidence and the use of modern observational-data methods to examine changes in preventive care following Medicaid expansion.
