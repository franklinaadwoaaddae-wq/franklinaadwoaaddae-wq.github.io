Research

Research Interests

My research interests center on the use of modern statistical methods to investigate substantive questions in public health, education, and policy. I am interested in causal inference, machine learning, and statistical computing for complex and large-scale data.

01

Causal Inference

I am interested in methods for estimating causal effects using observational and quasi-experimental data. A central part of this work is understanding how research design, identifying assumptions, and statistical methodology interact when evaluating policies and interventions.

Difference-in-Differences Event Studies Treatment Effects Observational Data Policy Evaluation

02

Machine Learning

I am interested in the intersection of statistical inference and machine learning, where flexible predictive methods can support questions involving heterogeneous, high-dimensional, or large-scale data.

Double Machine Learning Predictive Modeling High-Dimensional Data Statistical Learning

03

Public Health & Policy

I am interested in applying quantitative methods to questions involving health care access, preventive health behavior, public programs, and policy interventions. This area provides an important setting for connecting methodological work with questions that have direct practical implications.

Health Policy Preventive Care Health Care Access Population Data

04

Education & Large-Scale Data

Another developing area of my research focuses on educational data and the opportunities created by large-scale datasets. I am interested in how statistical and machine-learning methods can be used to investigate meaningful questions about educational outcomes, patterns, and processes.

Educational Data Large-Scale Data Student Outcomes Applied Machine Learning

This is an evolving area of my doctoral research, with particular interest in identifying questions where modern statistical and machine-learning methods can make useful contributions to educational research.

05

Statistical Computing

Reproducible statistical computing is an important part of how I approach research. I am interested in developing transparent workflows for data management, simulation, modeling, visualization, and reproducible analysis.

R SAS Simulation Reproducible Research GitHub LaTeX

APPROACH

How I Approach Research

Thoughtful Design

Start with a clear research question and align the design and statistical methods with what the data can support.

Reproducibility

Build transparent workflows that make the analytical process organized, traceable, and reproducible.

Practical Impact

Connect rigorous statistical analysis with substantive questions and communicate findings clearly.
